# Playbook: Core CSM & Account Management KPIs and Execution Framework

**Owner:** Senior Customer Success Manager / Strategic Account Manager  
**Portfolio Scope:** Mid-Market & Enterprise B2B SaaS  
**Purpose:** Define the core performance metrics, operating standards, and execution workflows used to drive customer adoption, retention, renewal performance, and account growth across a scalable customer portfolio.

---

## 1. KPI Management Philosophy

Customer Success and Account Management performance should be measured through customer outcomes and business results, not activity volume alone.

Meetings held, emails sent, tickets closed, and QBRs completed are useful operational indicators, but they do not independently prove that customers are realizing value or that recurring revenue is protected.

My approach connects three areas:

1. **Customer outcomes:** Are customers adopting the product and achieving the business objectives they purchased it for?
2. **Revenue performance:** Are accounts renewing, retaining revenue, and expanding when there is a legitimate business need?
3. **Execution quality:** Are customer risks, opportunities, and commitments being identified, documented, and acted on consistently?

The objective is to create a measurable operating system where every important KPI has a clear definition, an accountable owner, an appropriate review cadence, and a corresponding action when performance moves off track.

**Operating principle: Measure what matters, understand what is changing, and turn the insight into action.**

## 2. Core KPI Scorecard & Benchmarks

The following scorecard establishes a starting point for a B2B SaaS Customer Success and Account Management organization.

Targets are illustrative planning benchmarks, not universal industry standards. Actual targets should be calibrated to the company's business model, customer segments, contract structure, product maturity, and historical performance.

| KPI | Definition / Formula | Illustrative Target | Primary Leading Indicators |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) | (Starting ARR − Churned ARR − Contraction ARR) ÷ Starting ARR | 92–95%+ | Adoption, unresolved issues, renewal readiness, executive engagement |
| Net Revenue Retention (NRR) | (Starting ARR − Churn − Contraction + Expansion) ÷ Starting ARR | 105–115%+ | Seat utilization, product depth, business outcomes, expansion readiness |
| Logo Retention | Customers retained ÷ Customers eligible to renew | 90–95%+ | Customer sentiment, engagement, champion continuity, value realization |
| Product Adoption | Active eligible users ÷ Total eligible licensed users | 80%+ | Core workflow completion, feature adoption, usage consistency |
| Time to First Value (TTFV) | Days from contract signature to first verified customer outcome | Segment-specific | Handoff quality, kickoff completion, technical readiness, milestone completion |
| Customer Health | Weighted assessment of adoption, engagement, outcomes, and support sentiment | 85%+ Green as an illustrative portfolio objective | Usage changes, stakeholder engagement, support trends |
| Renewal Forecast Accuracy | Accuracy of forecasted renewal outcomes against actual results | 90%+ as an illustrative goal | Customer intent, decision-maker alignment, procurement progress |
| QBR / EBR Coverage | Completed reviews ÷ Reviews due for eligible strategic accounts | 90%+ per review cycle | Stakeholder attendance, preparation, documented outcomes |
| Expansion Revenue | Additional recurring revenue from existing customers | Set by business plan | Adoption depth, capacity utilization, additional use cases |
| Support Resolution Performance | Tickets resolved within agreed SLA ÷ Tickets eligible for SLA measurement | Based on contractual SLA | Ticket aging, escalation volume, recurring issues |

### KPI Definitions & Calculation Notes

**Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)**

Measures how much recurring revenue remains from an existing customer cohort after churn and contraction, excluding expansion.

`GRR = (Starting ARR - Churned ARR - Contraction ARR) / Starting ARR × 100`

GRR helps determine whether the existing customer base is maintaining its revenue before growth is considered.

**Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**

Measures how much recurring revenue remains after accounting for churn, contraction, and expansion.

`NRR = (Starting ARR - Churned ARR - Contraction ARR + Expansion ARR) / Starting ARR × 100`

NRR above 100% indicates that expansion within the existing customer base has more than offset churn and contraction.

**Logo Retention**

Measures the percentage of customers retained over a defined period.

`Logo Retention = (Starting Customer Count - Churned Customers) / Starting Customer Count × 100`

Customer counts should use a consistent cohort and renewal eligibility definition.

**Product Adoption & Seat Utilization**

Seat utilization provides a basic view of licensed capacity being used.

`Seat Utilization = Active Licensed Users / Total Licensed Seats × 100`

Seat utilization should be evaluated alongside core workflow completion, frequency of use, and adoption across relevant teams. A login alone does not demonstrate meaningful adoption.

**Time to First Value (TTFV)**

Measures the time between contract signature and the first verified outcome that matters to the customer.

Examples include:
- Completing a core business workflow.
- Launching the first operational team.
- Replacing a manual process with a product workflow.
- Achieving the first measurable efficiency improvement.

TTFV should be measured against the customer's agreed success criteria rather than an arbitrary product activity milestone.

## 3. How to Execute Against Each KPI

### A. GRR: Retention & Churn Prevention

**Objective:** Protect existing recurring revenue by identifying risk early and maintaining a consistent focus on customer value.

Execution:
- Review renewal exposure at 180, 120, 90, and 60 days before renewal.
- Monitor product adoption, stakeholder engagement, support trends, and customer sentiment.
- Identify accounts with declining usage or unresolved business concerns.
- Validate whether the customer is achieving the outcomes established during onboarding.
- Establish documented recovery plans for accounts showing meaningful risk.
- Multi-thread strategic accounts across daily users, operational leaders, and economic buyers.
- Coordinate with Support, Product, and Engineering when customer issues require cross-functional ownership.

**When performance moves off track:**
- Segment churn and contraction by customer size, product, reason, and renewal period.
- Identify recurring causes rather than treating every lost account as an isolated event.
- Review whether risks were identified early enough and whether recovery actions were completed.
- Adjust onboarding, enablement, or account engagement processes when patterns emerge.

**Success indicator:** Retention performance improves because customer value and relationship risks are addressed before renewal decisions.

### B. NRR: Expansion & Account Growth

**Objective:** Grow existing customer revenue through demonstrated value, deeper adoption, and consultative discovery.

Execution:
- Monitor seat utilization and product capacity.
- Identify additional departments, teams, and workflows that could benefit from the platform.
- Review feature adoption and opportunities for broader product usage.
- Use QBRs and executive conversations to understand upcoming business initiatives.
- Build expansion recommendations around documented customer needs and measurable outcomes.
- Partner with Sales or Account Management on pricing, packaging, commercial proposals, and negotiation.

**Illustrative expansion signals:**
- An account consistently reaches 80% or more of its licensed seat capacity.
- A customer begins introducing new teams or business units.
- Existing workflows are producing measurable results that support broader adoption.
- A customer requests capabilities associated with a higher product tier.
- An executive sponsor identifies a new operational initiative the platform could support.

An expansion signal is an invitation to investigate, not an automatic sales opportunity.

**When performance moves off track:**
- Review whether expansion opportunities are being identified early enough.
- Separate genuine customer demand from seller-driven assumptions.
- Examine whether adoption or unresolved service issues are preventing growth.
- Validate opportunity stage, customer intent, economic buyer alignment, and next steps.

**Success indicator:** Expansion follows demonstrated customer value rather than being pursued at the expense of retention.

### C. Product Adoption & Time to First Value

**Objective:** Help customers reach meaningful outcomes quickly and build consistent product usage into their daily operations.

Execution:
- Establish a clean Sales-to-CS handoff before kickoff.
- Capture business objectives, technical requirements, key stakeholders, and expected outcomes.
- Create an onboarding plan with milestones and clear ownership.
- Identify the customer's first critical workflow and prioritize getting it operational.
- Deliver role-specific training and reusable enablement resources.
- Monitor adoption by user group, department, and core workflow.
- Follow up when customers struggle to move beyond initial setup.

**When adoption moves off track:**
- Identify which users, teams, or workflows are underperforming.
- Determine whether the issue is technical, organizational, or related to enablement.
- Revisit the original success criteria and confirm whether they remain relevant.
- Deliver targeted training rather than repeating generic product demonstrations.
- Establish a measurable adoption milestone and follow-up date.

**Success indicator:** Customers move from implementation to repeatable product usage and can explain how the platform supports their business.

### D. Customer Health & Relationship Management

**Objective:** Maintain visibility into customer conditions and identify risks before they affect renewal performance.

Execution:
- Maintain current account health assessments.
- Review adoption, engagement, business outcomes, and support sentiment.
- Monitor changes in champion and executive sponsor relationships.
- Track open risks, customer commitments, and unresolved issues.
- Document the reason behind meaningful changes in health status.
- Assign an owner and next action to every material account risk.

A health score should be treated as a decision-support tool, not a substitute for customer conversations.

**When health deteriorates:**
- Validate the underlying signals.
- Identify the actual cause of the change.
- Determine the customer's business impact.
- Assign recovery ownership.
- Track progress against measurable milestones.

**Success indicator:** Account risks become visible, actionable, and consistently managed across the portfolio.

### E. Renewal Forecasting & Commercial Readiness

**Objective:** Build a reliable view of renewal outcomes and reduce last-minute surprises.

Execution:
- Maintain accurate renewal dates and contract values in CRM.
- Segment renewal opportunities by timing, value, and risk.
- Confirm customer intent through direct conversations.
- Identify decision-makers, procurement steps, legal requirements, and budget considerations.
- Document commercial risks and unresolved customer concerns.
- Review forecast changes with Sales or Account Management.
- Maintain realistic close dates and next steps.

**Renewal forecast categories:**

| Forecast Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| Commit | Strong customer confirmation, aligned decision-makers, and a credible path to completion |
| Likely / Best Case | Positive indicators exist, but one or more meaningful conditions remain unresolved |
| At Risk | Material adoption, relationship, financial, service, or commercial concerns threaten renewal |
| Closed | Renewal or non-renewal outcome has been confirmed and recorded |

Forecast categories should follow the organization's approved definitions. A CSM's positive relationship with a champion should not be treated as sufficient evidence of renewal commitment.

**Success indicator:** Leadership has a credible, current view of renewal exposure, customer intent, and commercial next steps.

### F. QBR / EBR Coverage & Effectiveness

**Objective:** Use business reviews to strengthen executive alignment, validate customer outcomes, and establish priorities for the next period.

Execution:
- Identify eligible strategic accounts and reviews due.
- Prepare product usage, adoption, support, and outcome data.
- Review progress against previous commitments.
- Connect product activity to customer business priorities.
- Discuss risks, upcoming initiatives, and opportunities.
- Confirm executive alignment and stakeholder ownership.
- Document decisions, actions, owners, and target dates.

A completed meeting is not automatically a successful QBR.

Measure both coverage and quality:
- Was the appropriate stakeholder present?
- Were customer business outcomes reviewed?
- Were risks and opportunities discussed?
- Were decisions documented?
- Did the meeting produce an actionable plan?

**Success indicator:** Business reviews create alignment and measurable follow-through rather than becoming routine status meetings.

## 4. CSM vs. Account Management: KPI Ownership

Customer Success and Account Management often share responsibility for customer outcomes, but their primary performance responsibilities should be clearly defined.

The exact division depends on the organization's structure. Some teams combine both functions; others separate post-sale relationship management from commercial ownership.

| Performance Area | Customer Success Focus | Account Management Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Retention | Adoption, value realization, relationship health, early risk identification | Renewal strategy, commercial alignment, contract continuity |
| Expansion | Identify use cases and validate customer needs | Develop commercial opportunities and close expansion |
| Adoption | Drive implementation, enablement, and workflow usage | Support growth conversations tied to adoption |
| Customer Health | Monitor signals and execute recovery plans | Incorporate health and commercial risk into account strategy |
| QBR / EBR | Present outcomes, adoption, and success plans | Align executive priorities, commercial strategy, and growth |
| Forecasting | Communicate customer sentiment and renewal risks | Maintain commercial forecast, opportunity stage, and close plan |

### Shared Accountability

Both functions should have visibility into:
- Customer health and renewal readiness.
- Executive sponsor relationships.
- Contract value and renewal dates.
- Open customer risks and escalations.
- Expansion opportunities.
- Documented customer outcomes.

The customer should experience one coordinated partnership, not separate teams competing for ownership.

## 5. Portfolio Segmentation & KPI Prioritization

A scalable operating model requires different engagement strategies for different customer segments.

Not every account should receive the same cadence or level of manual attention.

| Segment | Typical Characteristics | Engagement Model | Primary KPI Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise / Strategic | Complex workflows, multiple stakeholders, higher revenue exposure | Personalized success plans, executive reviews, multi-threaded relationships | GRR, NRR, executive alignment, adoption depth |
| Mid-Market | Growing teams, defined operational needs, moderate complexity | Scheduled business reviews, targeted enablement, adoption monitoring | Adoption, TTFV, GRR, expansion readiness |
| SMB / Scaled | Lower complexity, higher account volume, repeatable use cases | Digital enablement, automated communications, exception-based outreach | Logo retention, activation, product adoption, scaled engagement |

### Prioritization Framework

When managing a large portfolio, prioritize accounts using a combination of:

1. Revenue exposure.
2. Renewal proximity.
3. Customer health and adoption trends.
4. Severity of unresolved issues.
5. Strategic importance.
6. Expansion potential.
7. Likelihood that intervention will change the outcome.

For a scaled book of business, automation and standardized workflows can help surface accounts requiring attention while preserving personalized engagement for strategic and at-risk customers.

**Important:** Automation should help determine where a CSM spends time. It should not replace the judgment required to understand why a customer needs attention.

## 6. KPI Operating Rhythm

A consistent operating cadence turns metrics into daily execution.

### Daily: Customer & Risk Execution

- Review urgent customer escalations.
- Respond to time-sensitive customer needs.
- Follow up on outstanding commitments.
- Update important account activity and next steps.
- Coordinate with internal teams on customer-impacting issues.

### Weekly: Portfolio & Pipeline Review

**Monday**
- Review Red and Yellow accounts.
- Identify new adoption and engagement changes.
- Review renewals approaching within 90 days.
- Identify expansion signals and accounts requiring intervention.
- Establish priority accounts for the week.

**Wednesday**
- Review recovery plan progress.
- Follow up on onboarding and adoption milestones.
- Coordinate unresolved support or product issues.
- Confirm customer meetings and stakeholder engagement.

**Friday**
- Update CRM records and account plans.
- Validate next steps and ownership.
- Review changes in renewal and expansion forecasts.
- Prepare the next week's priorities.

### Monthly: Performance Review

Review:
- GRR and NRR trends.
- Logo retention and churn reasons.
- Product adoption and seat utilization.
- TTFV and onboarding progress.
- Customer health distribution.
- Renewal forecast and upcoming exposure.
- Expansion pipeline.
- QBR / EBR coverage.

The goal is to identify patterns and operational improvements, not simply report numbers.

### Quarterly: Strategic Performance & Planning

- Evaluate performance against quarterly targets.
- Review retention and expansion trends by segment.
- Analyze customer feedback and recurring friction.
- Assess onboarding effectiveness and time-to-value.
- Review strategic account plans.
- Identify process gaps requiring cross-functional attention.
- Establish next-quarter priorities and measurable objectives.

## 7. Forecasting & KPI Variance Management

A KPI dashboard should explain not only whether a target was achieved, but also why performance changed and what the team plans to do about it.

### Variance Review Framework

When a metric falls below target, use the following sequence:

**1. Identify the variance**

Determine which metric moved, by how much, and over what period.

**2. Segment the results**

Break performance down by customer size, industry, product, CSM, cohort, or renewal period where appropriate.

**3. Diagnose the underlying cause**

Review customer feedback, product usage, support trends, onboarding progress, and commercial activity.

**4. Determine ownership**

Identify whether the issue requires action from Customer Success, Account Management, Sales, Support, Product, Engineering, or leadership.

**5. Establish corrective action**

Assign specific actions, owners, milestones, and target dates.

**6. Measure improvement**

Review the next reporting period to determine whether the intervention improved the metric or whether the strategy needs to change.

### Example: Adoption Performance Below Target

**Illustrative scenario:** A customer segment reports 62% active seat utilization against an internal target of 80%.

Potential causes:
- Users were licensed but never fully onboarded.
- Teams are relying on manual processes.
- Product workflows are not aligned with daily operations.
- A champion left and enablement stalled.
- The original rollout did not include all intended departments.

Corrective actions:
- Identify inactive user groups.
- Review product activity and workflow completion.
- Conduct targeted customer discovery.
- Deliver role-specific training.
- Reconfirm rollout priorities with the customer.
- Reassess adoption after an agreed measurement period.

The goal is not to push usage numbers upward without context. It is to understand why adoption is low and help the customer realize more value.

This scenario is illustrative and does not represent a reported customer result.

## 8. KPI Dashboard & Reporting Standards

A useful KPI dashboard should give leadership and account owners a clear view of performance, risk, and required action.

### Executive Dashboard

| Reporting Area | Core Metrics | Management Question |
|---|---|---|
| Retention | GRR, NRR, logo retention, churn | Are we protecting and growing the existing customer base? |
| Adoption | Active users, seat utilization, workflow completion | Are customers using the product meaningfully? |
| Onboarding | TTFV, milestone completion, implementation delays | Are customers reaching value quickly? |
| Customer Health | Health distribution, risk movement, recovery status | Where should the team focus attention? |
| Renewals | Upcoming ARR, forecast category, renewal risk | What revenue is exposed and what is the path to renewal? |
| Expansion | Qualified pipeline, expansion ARR, conversion | Where is customer value creating legitimate growth opportunities? |
| Engagement | QBR coverage, executive participation, stakeholder depth | Are we maintaining the relationships required to sustain value? |
| Support | SLA performance, ticket aging, escalations | Are service issues affecting customer confidence? |

### Reporting Standards

- Use consistent metric definitions across teams.
- Establish a clear reporting period and customer cohort.
- Separate actual results from forecasts and targets.
- Document data sources and calculation methods.
- Avoid mixing account counts with revenue-weighted metrics.
- Identify missing or unreliable data.
- Show trends over time rather than relying on isolated snapshots.
- Pair material KPI changes with an explanation and action plan.

## 10. Cross-Functional KPI Accountability

Customer outcomes are influenced by multiple teams. KPI execution requires clear handoffs and shared visibility.

| Team | Primary Contribution |
|---|---|
| Customer Success | Adoption, value realization, customer health, success plans, retention risk |
| Account Management | Renewal strategy, commercial relationships, expansion, account growth |
| Sales | New customer expectations, qualification, accurate handoff information |
| Support | Issue resolution, SLA performance, customer-impacting escalations |
| Product | Product feedback, usability improvements, adoption blockers |
| Engineering | Technical reliability, integrations, defects, escalated technical issues |
| Leadership | Strategic priorities, resource allocation, executive alignment |

### Cross-Functional Review Standards

For significant customer risks or KPI variances:
- Define the customer and business impact.
- Identify the team responsible for the next action.
- Establish a clear owner and due date.
- Communicate customer-facing commitments.
- Escalate missed commitments when necessary.
- Document the resolution and any process improvements.

A KPI should never become an excuse for teams to shift responsibility. The operating model should make ownership clearer, not create silos.

## 11. Illustrative Quarterly KPI Review

**Scenario:** A mid-market and enterprise portfolio enters a quarterly review with retention performance near target, but adoption and expansion are below plan.

| KPI | Illustrative Target | Actual | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| GRR | 95% | 93% | Below Target |
| NRR | 110% | 106% | Below Target |
| Active Seat Utilization | 80% | 68% | Below Target |
| TTFV | 21 Days | 25 Days | Below Target |
| QBR Coverage | 90% | 94% | On Target |

These figures are hypothetical and demonstrate how a team might interpret a quarterly scorecard.

### Management Interpretation

**Retention:** GRR is below target, requiring a review of churn and contraction reasons.

**Expansion:** NRR is below target, suggesting that expansion is not sufficiently offsetting contraction and churn.

**Adoption:** Seat utilization is materially below target, indicating potential onboarding, enablement, or workflow challenges.

**Onboarding:** TTFV is slower than planned, suggesting implementation friction or gaps in the handoff process.

**QBR Coverage:** Review completion is above target, but meeting quality and follow-through should also be evaluated.

### Corrective Action Plan

| Priority | Action | Owner | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Analyze churn and contraction reasons by segment | CS / AM Leadership | GRR trend |
| 2 | Identify underutilized accounts and launch targeted adoption plans | CSMs | Active seat utilization |
| 3 | Review implementation delays and Sales-to-CS handoff quality | CS / Implementation | TTFV |
| 4 | Reassess expansion opportunities against verified customer outcomes | AM / Sales | Qualified expansion pipeline |
| 5 | Audit QBR action completion and executive alignment | CSMs / AMs | Completed customer commitments |

The purpose of the review is to move from reporting performance to improving it.

## 12. Operating Philosophy

My approach to KPI management is grounded in a few principles:

- **Measure outcomes, not activity alone.** Customer meetings and outreach matter when they lead to progress.
- **Retention and growth are connected.** Expansion should follow demonstrated value and customer confidence.
- **Use leading indicators to manage lagging results.** Churn and contraction are outcomes; adoption, engagement, and unresolved issues can provide earlier warning.
- **Segment before acting.** Different customer profiles require different engagement strategies.
- **Make metrics actionable.** Every meaningful variance should have an explanation, owner, and next step.
- **Keep teams aligned.** Customer Success and Account Management should share visibility into customer outcomes and commercial risk.
- **Maintain forecast discipline.** A realistic forecast is more valuable than an optimistic number without customer evidence.
- **Standardize the process, not the customer.** Consistent operating standards should still leave room for customer-specific needs.

**Final principle: Strong Customer Success and Account Management organizations do more than report performance. They build repeatable systems that connect customer value, operational execution, and recurring revenue growth.**
