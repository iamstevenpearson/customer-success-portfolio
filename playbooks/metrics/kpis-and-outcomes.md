# Playbook: Customer Health Scoring & Retention Framework

**Owner:** Senior Customer Success Manager / Strategic Account Manager  
**Portfolio Scope:** Mid-Market & Enterprise B2B SaaS  
**Purpose:** Establish a consistent, proactive approach to evaluating customer health, identifying early churn signals, prioritizing account engagement, and protecting recurring revenue through measurable customer outcomes.

---

## 1. Customer Health Scoring Philosophy

A customer health score should do more than assign an account a color. It should help a Customer Success Manager understand what is happening inside an account, identify why it is happening, and determine what action should happen next.

A healthy account is not necessarily one with frequent logins. It is one where customers are consistently realizing value, adopting the workflows that matter to their business, maintaining stakeholder alignment, and demonstrating confidence in the relationship.

My approach combines quantitative signals with qualitative customer context.

**Operating principle: Data tells me where to look. Customer conversations tell me why.**

Health scores should guide decisions, not replace judgment. A score is only useful when its underlying data is current, its risk indicators are understood, and an owner is accountable for the next action.

## 2. Weighted Customer Health Score Model

Each account receives a composite score on a 100-point scale across four categories.

The weights below represent an illustrative scoring model. They should be calibrated to the product, customer segment, available telemetry, and actual relationship risks.

| Health Category | Weight | Leading Indicators | Potential Risk Signals |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Product Adoption & Depth | 35% | Weekly active users, seat utilization, core workflow completion, feature adoption, usage consistency | Declining active usage, low seat utilization, abandoned workflows, limited adoption beyond initial users |
| Executive & Champion Engagement | 25% | Meeting participation, QBR attendance, stakeholder coverage, executive alignment, responsiveness | Champion departure, unanswered outreach, missed reviews, dependence on one relationship |
| Business Outcome Realization | 25% | Progress against success plans, documented ROI, operational improvements, milestone completion | Unclear success criteria, stalled initiatives, unverified value, inability to connect usage to business outcomes |
| Support & Sentiment Health | 15% | Support trends, resolution times, CSAT/NPS feedback, escalation history, billing status | Repeated unresolved issues, critical escalations, negative feedback, declining confidence |

### Scoring Method

Each category receives a score from 0 to 100.

The weighted health score is calculated as:

`Health Score = (Adoption × 0.35) + (Engagement × 0.25) + (Business Outcomes × 0.25) + (Support & Sentiment × 0.15)`

**Example:**

| Category | Category Score | Weight | Weighted Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Product Adoption | 80 | 35% | 28.0 |
| Executive Engagement | 60 | 25% | 15.0 |
| Business Outcomes | 75 | 25% | 18.75 |
| Support & Sentiment | 90 | 15% | 13.5 |
| **Total Health Score** | | | **75.25** |

The account would be classified as Watchlist based on the illustrative health bands below.

This example demonstrates the scoring method; it is not a reported customer result.

### Important Scoring Considerations

- A strong adoption score should not automatically override a serious executive relationship risk.
- High usage does not guarantee that the customer understands or recognizes business value.
- A single unresolved critical issue may justify immediate escalation even if the overall score remains Green.
- Missing or outdated data should be identified rather than automatically treated as positive health.
- Scores should be reviewed over time to identify direction and momentum, not just a single point-in-time result.

## 3. Health Levels & Required Action Plans

| Score | Health Status | Engagement Cadence | Primary Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80–100 | Healthy (Green) | Monthly strategic check-in and quarterly business review | Reinforce value, maintain stakeholder alignment, identify opportunities for deeper adoption |
| 60–79 | Watchlist (Yellow) | Biweekly targeted engagement and adoption follow-up | Identify the source of declining health, address workflow gaps, and document recovery milestones |
| 0–59 | At Risk (Red) | Weekly recovery engagement and internal escalation review | Establish a recovery owner, diagnose root causes, and execute a documented stabilization plan |

These thresholds are starting points for an operating model, not universal industry benchmarks.

### Green: Protect and Build on Value

The objective is to maintain customer confidence while continuing to improve outcomes.

Actions:
- Confirm that the customer is achieving the outcomes established during onboarding.
- Review adoption depth and identify workflows that could deliver additional value.
- Maintain relationships with both day-to-day users and executive stakeholders.
- Document measurable customer wins and progress against success criteria.
- Identify potential expansion opportunities only when supported by demonstrated value.

**Desired outcome:** A customer who understands the value of the partnership and has a clear reason to continue investing.

### Yellow: Diagnose and Stabilize

The objective is to identify emerging issues before they become renewal risks.

Actions:
- Compare current usage against the account's historical baseline.
- Identify specific workflows, teams, or user groups experiencing adoption challenges.
- Review open support issues and recurring customer complaints.
- Reconnect with inactive champions and engage secondary stakeholders.
- Schedule targeted enablement based on actual workflow gaps.
- Create a short recovery plan with owners, milestones, and follow-up dates.

**Desired outcome:** A clear understanding of the problem and measurable progress toward restoring customer confidence.

### Red: Execute a Recovery Plan

The objective is to stabilize the relationship, resolve critical issues, and establish a credible path to renewed value.

Actions:
- Confirm the primary reason for account deterioration.
- Assign an internal recovery owner and involve leadership when appropriate.
- Engage the executive sponsor and relevant operational stakeholders.
- Coordinate with Support, Product, Engineering, or Operations to address blockers.
- Establish weekly customer communication and internal escalation reviews.
- Document recovery milestones and communicate progress transparently.
- Reassess account health based on verified improvements rather than promises.

**Desired outcome:** A stabilized customer relationship with documented progress, clear accountability, and a realistic retention path.

## 4. Leading Indicators & Early Warning Signals

The strongest retention programs identify risk before the customer formally communicates an intention to leave.

I monitor changes in customer behavior, stakeholder engagement, and realized value rather than waiting for a cancellation notice or renewal conversation.

| Signal | What It May Indicate | Recommended Response |
|---|---|---|
| 20%+ decline in weekly active usage | Adoption friction or reduced product relevance | Review usage patterns and contact the customer to understand the change |
| Seat utilization below 60% | Unused capacity or incomplete rollout | Identify inactive teams and offer targeted enablement |
| Primary champion inactive for 14–21 days | Relationship deterioration or internal transition | Reach out and establish additional stakeholder relationships |
| Repeated missed meetings | Reduced engagement or competing priorities | Reassess communication approach and confirm current priorities |
| Critical support issue unresolved | Operational disruption and declining confidence | Coordinate escalation ownership and provide proactive updates |
| Success milestones repeatedly delayed | Customer value is not being realized | Revisit the success plan and identify the actual blocker |
| Executive sponsor disengagement | Reduced strategic alignment | Reconnect with leadership and validate business priorities |
| Renewal approaching with no documented value | Weak renewal justification | Conduct a value review and address unresolved concerns |

**Important:** These are configurable warning thresholds, not automatic proof of churn risk. Every signal should be validated against customer context.

## 5. Portfolio Prioritization & Weekly Health Review

Health scoring becomes especially valuable when a CSM manages a large book of business.

With a portfolio of approximately 350 accounts, it is not practical to give every customer the same level of attention. The goal is to establish a repeatable prioritization process that directs time toward the accounts with the greatest risk, opportunity, and strategic importance.

### Account Prioritization Framework

| Priority | Account Profile | Engagement Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1 | Red health, strategic revenue exposure, imminent renewal, or critical escalation | Personalized recovery plan, frequent follow-up, cross-functional coordination |
| Priority 2 | Yellow health, declining adoption, stakeholder changes, or unresolved value gaps | Targeted outreach, enablement, stakeholder re-engagement |
| Priority 3 | Green strategic accounts with growth potential | Executive alignment, business reviews, adoption expansion |
| Priority 4 | Stable, lower-complexity accounts with consistent adoption | Scaled communication, automated education, scheduled health checks |

Prioritization should consider more than the health score alone.

Additional factors include:
- Contract value and revenue exposure.
- Days remaining until renewal.
- Severity and duration of customer issues.
- Strategic importance of the account.
- Potential business impact of unresolved adoption gaps.
- Availability and engagement of decision-makers.
- Likelihood that intervention can meaningfully change the outcome.

A smaller strategic account with a critical renewal risk may deserve more immediate attention than a larger account with stable adoption and strong executive alignment.

### Weekly Portfolio Triage

**Monday: Review and Prioritize**
- Review Red and Yellow accounts.
- Identify new changes in usage, engagement, and support trends.
- Review renewals approaching within 180 days.
- Identify accounts with recent champion or executive changes.
- Assign priority levels and confirm next actions.

**Tuesday–Wednesday: Customer Engagement**
- Conduct targeted outreach to priority accounts.
- Schedule adoption and recovery conversations.
- Coordinate with internal teams on outstanding customer issues.
- Deliver enablement based on identified needs.

**Thursday: Progress & Forecast Review**
- Review recovery plan milestones.
- Validate whether customer engagement and adoption are improving.
- Update renewal risk assessments.
- Escalate unresolved blockers.

**Friday: Documentation & Forward Planning**
- Update CRM account notes and health scores.
- Confirm owners and next action dates.
- Review accounts requiring leadership attention.
- Prepare the following week's engagement priorities.

The exact cadence should adapt to portfolio size, customer complexity, renewal timing, and escalation volume.

## 6. Outcome-Driven Renewal Runway: 180-Day Countdown

Renewal readiness should be developed throughout the customer lifecycle, not treated as a last-minute commercial exercise.

### T-180 Days: Value Audit & Stakeholder Mapping

**Objective:** Understand the customer's current health and identify potential renewal risks early.

Actions:
- Review historical product usage and adoption trends.
- Validate progress against the original business objectives.
- Review support history and unresolved customer concerns.
- Confirm executive sponsor and champion relationships.
- Identify gaps in stakeholder coverage.
- Document early renewal risks and assign follow-up actions.

**Deliverable:** Updated account health assessment and renewal readiness plan.

### T-120 Days: Executive Value Review

**Objective:** Demonstrate the value delivered and address gaps before commercial discussions intensify.

Actions:
- Prepare a concise executive value summary.
- Connect verified product adoption to measurable customer outcomes.
- Review progress against agreed success milestones.
- Address unresolved product, support, or relationship concerns.
- Confirm upcoming business priorities and organizational changes.

**Deliverable:** Executive value summary and documented action plan.

### T-90 Days: Renewal Risk & Commercial Alignment

**Objective:** Establish a realistic renewal forecast and remove outstanding obstacles.

Actions:
- Confirm the customer's renewal intent and decision-making process.
- Validate contract scope, seat utilization, and expected requirements.
- Review open risks and recovery milestones.
- Partner with Account Management or Sales on commercial strategy.
- Confirm economic buyer alignment and procurement requirements.
- Update CRM with forecast status, risks, and next steps.

**Deliverable:** Updated renewal forecast with documented customer alignment and ownership.

### T-30 Days: Finalize & Transition

**Objective:** Complete the renewal while maintaining customer momentum.

Actions:
- Confirm remaining legal, procurement, and signature requirements.
- Resolve any outstanding administrative or commercial issues.
- Confirm the next success period's business objectives.
- Update the account plan and stakeholder map.
- Schedule the next strategic review.

**Deliverable:** Completed renewal and updated Mutual Success Plan.

## 7. Health Score Governance & CRM Documentation

A health score is only as reliable as the information behind it.

Customer Success teams should establish clear documentation standards so that account health can be understood consistently across CSMs, Account Managers, leadership, and cross-functional teams.

### Required Account Records

Every actively managed account should have:

- Current health score and status.
- Last health review date.
- Product adoption and engagement trends.
- Key customer objectives and success milestones.
- Executive sponsor and primary champion.
- Open support issues and escalation history.
- Renewal date and current renewal risk.
- Documented risks and opportunities.
- Named internal owner and next action date.

### Health Score Review Standards

- Review health scores at a consistent cadence.
- Recalculate scores when meaningful customer signals change.
- Document why an account moved between health categories.
- Validate automated alerts before initiating customer-facing escalation.
- Avoid leaving outdated scores in place when account conditions materially change.
- Maintain consistent definitions across the portfolio.

### Technology & Data Sources

A health scoring program may draw from multiple systems:

| System | Primary Use |
|---|---|
| Salesforce / HubSpot | Account records, renewal dates, stakeholder information, activity history |
| Gainsight / Vitally | Customer health, success plans, lifecycle management, risk workflows |
| Looker | Product usage trends, adoption reporting, portfolio analysis |
| Zendesk | Support volume, escalation history, resolution trends |
| AI / Automation Tools | Signal summarization, risk alerts, account research, workflow support |

The specific tools depend on the organization's technology stack. Integrations and automated scoring should only be represented as implemented when they have actually been configured and validated.

## 8. Cross-Functional Retention Ownership

Customer retention is not the responsibility of Customer Success alone.

A CSM may own the customer relationship and recovery plan, but successful execution often requires coordination across multiple teams.

| Team | Contribution to Retention |
|---|---|
| Customer Success | Account health, customer communication, success planning, adoption, and recovery ownership |
| Account Management / Sales | Commercial strategy, renewal negotiation, contract changes, and expansion alignment |
| Support | Issue investigation, troubleshooting, escalation management, and resolution |
| Product | Product feedback, feature gaps, usability improvements, and roadmap alignment |
| Engineering | Technical investigation, defects, integrations, and platform reliability |
| Leadership | Executive alignment, strategic escalation, and additional organizational support |

### Escalation Communication Standards

When a customer issue requires cross-functional support:

1. Document the customer impact, business urgency, and issue history.
2. Identify the internal team responsible for investigation.
3. Assign a clear owner and expected follow-up date.
4. Communicate progress to the customer before they have to ask.
5. Escalate when commitments are missed or business impact increases.
6. Confirm resolution with the customer and document the outcome.

Internal coordination should never leave the customer wondering who owns the problem.

## 9. Measuring Retention Program Effectiveness

The purpose of health scoring is not simply to produce a dashboard. It is to improve the quality and timing of customer decisions.

The following metrics help evaluate whether the operating model is working:

| Metric | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) | Revenue retained before expansion |
| Net Revenue Retention (NRR) | Revenue retained and expanded across the existing customer base |
| Health Score Distribution | Percentage of accounts classified as Green, Yellow, and Red |
| Risk Identification Lead Time | How early potential renewal risks are identified |
| Risk Recovery Rate | Percentage of at-risk accounts that return to healthier status |
| Renewal Forecast Accuracy | Alignment between forecasted and actual renewal outcomes |
| Adoption Trend | Changes in active usage, seat utilization, and core workflow completion |
| Stakeholder Coverage | Depth and continuity of customer relationships |
| Recovery Plan Completion | Percentage of documented recovery milestones completed on time |

Metrics should be evaluated over time and segmented by customer size, product, contract value, and account complexity wherever possible.

A Red account moving to Green is a useful signal, but it should not automatically be counted as a successful retention outcome until customer value and renewal status are validated.

## 10. Illustrative Account Health Scenario

**Scenario: Enterprise Account Showing Early Renewal Risk**

An enterprise customer has historically maintained strong product adoption, but recent reporting reveals a decline in weekly active users. The primary champion has also stopped attending monthly check-ins, and a critical support issue remains unresolved.

The account's overall health score is still within the Yellow range.

### Step 1: Identify

Review product usage, meeting attendance, support history, and stakeholder activity to identify what has changed.

### Step 2: Diagnose

Contact the customer to understand whether the adoption decline is related to organizational changes, workflow friction, unresolved technical issues, or shifting business priorities.

### Step 3: Prioritize

Assess the renewal date, revenue exposure, severity of the support issue, and level of executive engagement.

### Step 4: Engage

Reconnect with the customer, establish the current business priorities, and identify the right operational and executive stakeholders.

### Step 5: Act

Coordinate with Support and Product on the unresolved issue, provide targeted enablement, and establish a recovery plan with measurable milestones.

### Step 6: Measure

Track usage recovery, stakeholder participation, issue resolution, and progress against the customer's business objectives.

### Step 7: Sustain

Once the account stabilizes, maintain an agreed engagement cadence and incorporate the customer's updated objectives into the next success plan.

**Key takeaway:** The goal is not to change an account's health color as quickly as possible. The goal is to address the underlying conditions that caused the risk.

This is an illustrative scenario demonstrating the framework, not a claim about a specific customer engagement.

## 11. Operating Philosophy

My approach to customer retention is built around a few consistent principles:

- **Lead with signals, validate with conversations.** Usage and engagement data reveal changes, but customers provide the context behind them.
- **Retention starts before renewal.** Customer value, adoption, and executive alignment should be established throughout the relationship.
- **Prioritize based on risk and impact.** Not every account requires the same level of attention at the same time.
- **Stabilize the relationship before trying to expand it.** Growth conversations should follow demonstrated value and customer confidence.
- **Build relationships beyond a single champion.** Multi-threading protects continuity when customer stakeholders change.
- **Make every risk actionable.** A health score without an owner, next step, and follow-up date is just a number.
- **Measure outcomes, not activity alone.** More meetings and emails do not necessarily mean stronger customer relationships.

**Final principle: A strong retention strategy does not wait for customers to announce that something is wrong. It creates the visibility, accountability, and customer relationships needed to recognize problems early and act before they become renewal decisions.**
