# Playbook: 30-60-90 Day Customer Onboarding & Time-to-Value (SMB to Enterprise)

**Owner:** Senior Customer Success Manager / Strategic Account Manager  
**Portfolio Scope:** SMB, Mid-Market & Enterprise B2B SaaS  
**Focus Areas:** Customer Onboarding, Implementation, Adoption, Time-to-Value, Stakeholder Alignment & Retention

## Purpose

Establish a repeatable, right-sized implementation framework designed to accelerate time-to-value, standardize Sales-to-CS handoffs, and establish early stakeholder alignment across high-velocity SMB teams and complex enterprise accounts.

The objective is to move customers from contract signature to meaningful product adoption through structured implementation, role-based enablement, measurable milestones, and proactive engagement.

My approach emphasizes three priorities:

1. **Establish Alignment:** Understand customer objectives, expectations, stakeholders, and desired outcomes before implementation begins.
2. **Accelerate Adoption:** Remove implementation barriers and help customers integrate the product into their daily workflows.
3. **Demonstrate Value:** Measure progress against agreed-upon business objectives and establish a foundation for long-term customer success.

---

## 1. Segment Calibration Matrix

Not every customer requires the same onboarding experience. Engagement should reflect account complexity, contract value, technical requirements, and available resources.

| Segment | Kickoff Window | Engagement Cadence | Primary Success Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SMB / Commercial** | Within 3 business days | Async digital enablement + 2 live milestone checkpoints | Core workflow live in under 14 days; 80%+ active seat utilization |
| **Mid-Market** | Within 5 business days | Weekly implementation calls + Day 30 sponsor pulse | Primary integration validated; repeatable team playbooks active |
| **Enterprise** | Within 5 business days | Formal project plan, steering committee & executive sponsor sync | Multi-department rollout, SSO/API production sign-off & ROI baseline |

*Note: Timelines and adoption thresholds are illustrative operating targets and should be calibrated to product complexity, customer readiness, and contractual commitments.*

## 2. Phase One: Days 1–30 — Foundation, Handoff & First Value

**Primary Objective:** Establish alignment, remove implementation barriers, and get the customer to their first meaningful product outcome.

### Sales-to-CS Handoff (T-48 Hours)

Before kickoff, review:

- CRM deal notes and commercial terms.
- Promised business outcomes and customer expectations.
- Technical requirements and integration dependencies.
- Key stakeholders, decision-makers, and executive sponsors.
- Contract scope, implementation commitments, and potential risks.

The goal is to ensure the customer does not have to repeat information already shared during the sales process.

### Executive Kickoff Call

Establish a shared understanding of what success looks like.

Key discussion points:

- Customer business objectives and desired outcomes.
- Current operational challenges.
- Implementation scope and ownership.
- Measurable success criteria.
- Key milestones and target completion dates.
- Communication expectations and escalation paths.

Document success criteria with a defined owner, target metric, and deadline.

### Technical & Data Readiness

Coordinate implementation requirements, including:

- Workspace configuration and permissions.
- Sandbox-to-production integration validation.
- Data ingestion and quality checks.
- SSO/API requirements where applicable.
- Technical dependencies and internal resource assignments.

For complex enterprise accounts, maintain a documented implementation plan with clear ownership and milestone tracking.

### Role-Based Enablement

Training should reflect how different customer stakeholders will actually use the platform.

**Administrators:**
- Workspace configuration.
- User permissions and access.
- Reporting and administrative controls.

**End Users:**
- Core workflows and daily platform usage.
- Relevant features and functionality.
- Common troubleshooting scenarios.

**Executive Sponsors:**
- Business objectives and success metrics.
- Adoption visibility and reporting.
- Expected operational outcomes.

### Day 30 Sponsor Check-In

Review:

- Implementation progress.
- Initial adoption signals.
- Outstanding technical dependencies.
- Training completion.
- Early friction points.
- Readiness to transition from technical setup into broader adoption.

**Deliverable:** Documented implementation status, initial adoption baseline, and agreed-upon next steps.

## 3. Phase Two: Days 31–60 — Workflow Adoption & Habit Formation

**Primary Objective:** Move beyond initial implementation and establish consistent product usage across the customer's organization.

### Telemetry & Usage Audit

Review available product analytics to evaluate:

- Active user rates.
- Feature adoption and usage depth.
- Engagement frequency.
- Department-level utilization.
- Under-adopted teams and workflows.
- Automated customer health alerts.

Use this information to identify adoption gaps before they become long-term retention concerns.

### Champion Enablement

Identify internal power users who can reinforce adoption across the organization.

Provide champions with:

- Quick-reference guides.
- Internal SOP templates.
- Role-specific training materials.
- Product updates and enablement resources.
- Defined escalation and feedback channels.

The goal is to create internal advocates who help adoption scale beyond direct CSM involvement.

### Process Standardization

Help customers embed platform workflows into their existing operating procedures.

Rather than treating the product as another isolated login, identify opportunities to incorporate it into recurring business activities, team meetings, and established processes.

### Mid-Point Risk Check

Evaluate:

- Unresolved support escalations.
- Adoption gaps.
- Training completion.
- Stakeholder engagement.
- Integration performance.
- Progress against implementation milestones.

Develop corrective actions for any areas falling behind expectations.

**Deliverable:** Adoption assessment, documented gaps, champion engagement plan, and updated customer success milestones.

---

## 4. Phase Three: Days 61–90 — Value Realization & Executive Alignment

**Primary Objective:** Demonstrate measurable progress, establish long-term account ownership, and transition the customer into ongoing Customer Success management.

### 90-Day Value & ROI Readout

Compare actual performance against the objectives established during kickoff.

Potential measurement areas include:

- Time saved through workflow automation.
- Improvement in process completion rates.
- Increased product adoption.
- Reduced manual administrative work.
- Operational efficiency improvements.
- Progress toward customer-defined business objectives.

Where baseline data is available, quantify the improvements rather than relying exclusively on anecdotal feedback.

### Executive Alignment

Reconnect with the executive sponsor to review:

- Original business objectives.
- Implementation outcomes.
- Adoption performance.
- Outstanding challenges.
- Additional opportunities to improve operational value.

Confirm that the customer recognizes the value delivered and understands the next phase of the partnership.

### Graduation to Steady-State Customer Success

Transition from implementation-focused engagement into ongoing lifecycle management.

Ensure the following are documented:

- Customer health baseline.
- Key stakeholders and relationship map.
- Adoption trends and opportunities.
- Open support issues.
- Renewal timeline.
- Success plan and future milestones.
- Agreed engagement cadence.

### First QBR Scheduled

Schedule the inaugural Quarterly Business Review with the appropriate customer stakeholders.

Establish a forward-looking agenda focused on business outcomes, adoption, customer feedback, and opportunities for continued growth.

**Deliverable:** Completed onboarding assessment, 90-day value readout, updated success plan, and ongoing engagement strategy.

## 5. Stakeholder Management & Communication Strategy

Successful onboarding requires alignment across multiple customer and internal stakeholders.

| Stakeholder | Primary Responsibility | Engagement Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Executive Sponsor** | Strategic alignment and business outcomes | ROI, organizational goals, and progress |
| **Primary Champion** | Internal adoption and coordination | Enablement, engagement, and feedback |
| **Technical Stakeholders** | Integrations and technical readiness | Configuration, data, and troubleshooting |
| **End Users** | Daily platform adoption | Training, usability, and workflow execution |
| **Internal Sales Team** | Commercial expectations and handoff | Contract scope and commitments |
| **Support / Implementation Teams** | Technical execution and issue resolution | Escalations, dependencies, and delivery |

For enterprise customers, maintain a clear communication structure so that decisions, responsibilities, and implementation blockers remain visible.

## 6. Measuring Onboarding Effectiveness

Onboarding should be measured by the customer's ability to achieve meaningful outcomes, not simply by whether implementation tasks have been completed.

| KPI | What It Measures |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Time to First Value (TTFV)** | Time required for the customer to achieve their first meaningful outcome |
| **Time to Go-Live** | Time required to complete implementation and launch core workflows |
| **Activation Rate** | Percentage of customers completing defined onboarding milestones |
| **Active Seat Utilization** | Percentage of purchased or available seats actively being used |
| **Feature Adoption** | Engagement with core product capabilities |
| **Training Completion** | Completion of required administrator and end-user enablement |
| **Implementation Milestone Completion** | Progress against agreed-upon project timelines |
| **90-Day Customer Health** | Overall adoption, engagement, support, and relationship indicators |

The objective is to establish baseline performance, monitor progress, identify friction points, and continuously improve the onboarding experience.

## 7. Practical Scenario: Enterprise Customer Implementation

**Scenario:** An enterprise customer purchases a SaaS platform intended to support multiple departments across a distributed organization.

The customer has several technical dependencies, multiple stakeholder groups, and an executive sponsor expecting measurable operational improvements within the first 90 days.

### Initial Challenge

The organization needs to coordinate implementation across departments while ensuring employees are properly trained and the platform becomes part of their daily workflows.

### CSM Approach

**Days 1–30: Establish the Foundation**
- Complete the Sales-to-CS handoff.
- Map stakeholders and identify the executive sponsor.
- Establish implementation ownership and milestones.
- Coordinate technical requirements.
- Deliver role-based training.
- Validate initial workflows.

**Days 31–60: Drive Adoption**
- Monitor department-level usage.
- Identify under-adopted teams.
- Engage internal champions.
- Deliver targeted enablement.
- Address implementation and support blockers.

**Days 61–90: Demonstrate Value**
- Review adoption and performance trends.
- Compare outcomes against initial objectives.
- Present progress to the executive sponsor.
- Finalize the ongoing success plan.
- Identify additional opportunities for platform utilization.

### Desired Outcomes

- Successful implementation of agreed-upon core workflows.
- Increased employee adoption.
- Improved stakeholder engagement.
- Documented progress toward business objectives.
- Established executive alignment.
- Successful transition into ongoing Customer Success management.

*This is an illustrative scenario demonstrating how the onboarding framework can be applied to a complex enterprise implementation.*

## 8. Strategic Operating Philosophy

My approach to onboarding is built around six principles:

1. **Align:** Establish expectations, objectives, and stakeholder ownership before implementation begins.
2. **Understand:** Learn the customer's workflows, challenges, and desired business outcomes.
3. **Implement:** Coordinate technical requirements and remove barriers to launch.
4. **Enable:** Equip administrators, champions, and end users with the knowledge to succeed.
5. **Measure:** Track adoption, time-to-value, and progress against agreed-upon success criteria.
6. **Sustain:** Transition into ongoing relationship management and continuous value realization.

**Bottom Line:** Successful onboarding is not simply about getting customers live. It is about helping them achieve meaningful value quickly, establishing habits that drive long-term adoption, and creating the foundation for a lasting customer relationship.
