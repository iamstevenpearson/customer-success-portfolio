# Sales-to-CS Handoff Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

> **Purpose:** Establish a consistent post-sale transition process that transfers commercial context, customer expectations, technical requirements, and success criteria from Sales to Customer Success. The objective is to prevent context loss, eliminate repeated discovery questions, establish clear ownership, and accelerate time-to-value across SMB, mid-market, and enterprise accounts.

---

## 1. Operating Principles

A successful handoff is more than transferring an account record from Sales to Customer Success. It is the transfer of the customer relationship, the commitments made during the sales process, and the business outcomes the customer expects to achieve.

The following principles guide the process:

- **Preserve customer context:** Customers should not have to repeat information already shared during discovery, evaluation, or negotiation.
- **Transfer commitments accurately:** Customer Success must understand what was promised, what was contracted, and what still requires validation.
- **Establish ownership early:** Every account should have a clearly assigned post-sale owner and an identified implementation path.
- **Connect the sale to measurable outcomes:** The handoff should establish why the customer purchased and how success will be evaluated.
- **Identify risk before kickoff:** Commercial, technical, organizational, and timeline risks should be surfaced before they affect implementation.
- **Create continuity:** The customer should experience a coordinated transition rather than a disconnect between Sales and the post-sale team.

## 2. Handoff Timeline & Ownership

| Milestone | Recommended Timing | Primary Owner | Deliverable / Completion Gate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deal Stage: Commit / Closed-Won** | T-0 (Signature / Closed-Won) | Account Executive (AE) | CRM notification, completed handoff intake, and signed contract attached or accessible. |
| **CS Assignment & Intake Review** | Within 1 business day | CS Manager / Assigned CSM | Account ownership assigned, CRM record reviewed, and missing information identified. |
| **Internal Handoff Sync** | Within 24–48 business hours | AE + Assigned CSM | Internal alignment completed, stakeholder map confirmed, and outstanding commitments documented. |
| **Customer Warm Introduction** | Within 48 business hours | AE + CSM | Joint introduction email sent and kickoff scheduling initiated. |
| **Implementation Readiness Review** | Before customer kickoff | CSM + Implementation / Technical Team | Technical dependencies, resources, responsibilities, and launch expectations reviewed. |
| **Customer Kickoff** | Within 3–5 business days, where feasible | CSM | Customer objectives confirmed, initial success milestones established, and next steps documented. |
| **Post-Kickoff Handoff Confirmation** | Within 1 business day after kickoff | CSM | CRM updated, action items assigned, risks documented, and ownership confirmed. |

*Timing should be adjusted for contract complexity, customer availability, implementation requirements, and internal resource capacity.*

## 3. Mandatory CRM Intake Checklist (AE Gate)

Before the internal handoff meeting, the Account Executive must document the following information in Salesforce, HubSpot, or the organization's designated CRM.

The assigned CSM should review the information before the handoff discussion.

### A. Commercial & Contract Information

- ARR / ACV and total contract value, where applicable.
- Contract start date and renewal date.
- Contract duration and billing cadence.
- Purchased products, modules, license tier, and seat count.
- Contracted usage limits and service entitlements.
- Support tier and any non-standard SLA requirements.
- Discounting, special pricing, and commercial concessions.
- Opt-out clauses, termination provisions, and renewal conditions.
- Outstanding contractual or procurement requirements.

### B. Business Drivers & Customer Pain Points

- Primary business problems that triggered the purchase.
- Current customer workflows and operational challenges.
- Why the customer decided to move forward now.
- Existing solutions, competitors, or manual processes being replaced.
- Business metrics the customer wants to improve.
- Executive-level objectives and expected business impact.
- Consequences of failing to achieve the desired outcomes.

### C. Promised Outcomes & Scope

- Explicit deliverables discussed during the sales process.
- Expected implementation and go-live timelines.
- Custom integrations or configuration requirements.
- Data migration or historical data requirements.
- SSO, API, security, or compliance dependencies.
- Custom reporting or dashboard expectations.
- Training and enablement commitments.
- Any verbal commitments that need written clarification.
- Items that were discussed but are not included in the contract.

**Important:** Sales commitments must be distinguished from confirmed product capabilities and contracted deliverables. Unvalidated requests should be documented as open items, not communicated as guaranteed outcomes.

### D. Stakeholder & Buying Committee Map

| Stakeholder | Role | Information to Capture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Economic Buyer | Financial decision-maker | Business justification, budget ownership, and ROI expectations. |
| Executive Sponsor | Senior internal advocate | Strategic priorities, executive visibility, and desired outcomes. |
| Day-to-Day Champion | Primary operational contact | Adoption ownership, internal coordination, and workflow requirements. |
| Technical / IT Lead | Technical stakeholder | Integration requirements, security, data access, and implementation dependencies. |
| End Users | Product users | Team size, workflows, training requirements, and adoption considerations. |
| Billing / Procurement Contact | Commercial administration | Invoicing, purchasing requirements, and contract administration. |

Not every account will have all stakeholder roles identified at the time of handoff. Missing contacts should be documented with an owner and follow-up action.

### E. Known Risks & Red Flags

- Aggressive or unrealistic go-live deadlines.
- Unresolved technical or integration questions.
- Limited executive sponsor involvement.
- Customer expectations that exceed contracted scope.
- Change-management resistance or low internal adoption readiness.
- Competing internal projects or organizational priorities.
- Unconfirmed budget or procurement dependencies.
- Outstanding product requests or roadmap expectations.
- Implementation resources that have not been assigned.
- Concerns or objections raised during the sales cycle.

Each identified risk should include its potential impact, current status, and next action.

## 4. Internal Handoff Agenda (20 Minutes)

The purpose of the internal handoff is to transfer the context necessary for Customer Success to take ownership without restarting the discovery process.

### 1. Why They Bought (5 Minutes)

**Owner:** Account Executive

Discuss:

- What business problem prompted the purchase?
- Why did the customer choose the solution?
- What created urgency?
- Which business outcomes matter most?
- What does the customer consider a successful first 90 days?
- What ROI or operational improvements were discussed?

**Expected outcome:** The CSM understands the customer's business objectives and the reason behind the purchase.

### 2. Stakeholders & Relationship Dynamics (5 Minutes)

**Owner:** Account Executive + CSM

Discuss:

- Who is the Economic Buyer?
- Who is the Executive Sponsor?
- Who will own day-to-day adoption?
- Who influenced the purchase decision?
- Were there skeptics or competing internal stakeholders?
- How engaged is the executive sponsor?
- Are there any relationship sensitivities or communication preferences?

**Expected outcome:** The CSM understands the buying committee, relationship dynamics, and stakeholder engagement strategy.

### 3. Technical & Scope Validation (5 Minutes)

**Owner:** Account Executive + CSM + Technical / Implementation Representative, when needed

Review:

- Products and services purchased.
- Implementation scope and dependencies.
- Data migration requirements.
- SSO, API, and integration requirements.
- Security or compliance considerations.
- Custom configuration requests.
- Solutions Engineering commitments.
- Outstanding technical questions.

**Expected outcome:** The implementation team understands what was sold, what is required, and what still needs validation.

### 4. Kickoff Strategy & Customer Expectations (5 Minutes)

**Owner:** Assigned CSM

Align on:

- Customer kickoff objectives.
- Initial success milestones.
- Required customer participants.
- Outstanding questions or unresolved commitments.
- Communication cadence.
- Immediate next steps.
- Potential risks requiring early intervention.

**Expected outcome:** Sales and CS agree on a consistent customer-facing message and a coordinated transition plan.

## 5. Handoff Completion & Acceptance Criteria

The handoff is not complete simply because a CRM notification was generated or a meeting occurred.

The assigned CSM should confirm that the following requirements are satisfied:

| Requirement | Responsible Party | Completion Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Commercial details documented | AE | Contract terms and purchased scope are accessible in CRM. |
| Business objectives recorded | AE | Primary customer goals and expected outcomes are documented. |
| Stakeholders identified | AE / CSM | Known decision-makers and operational contacts are mapped. |
| Sales commitments reviewed | AE / CSM | Promises, dependencies, and unresolved scope questions are documented. |
| Technical requirements reviewed | AE / Technical Team | Known integration and implementation dependencies are visible. |
| Risks documented | AE / CSM | Known risks have an owner and next action. |
| CSM assigned | CS Manager | A named account owner is recorded. |
| Customer introduction completed | AE / CSM | Customer receives a coordinated introduction. |

### Missing Information or Incomplete Handoffs

If critical information is missing:

1. The CSM documents the missing information in the CRM.
2. The CSM identifies whether the gap affects implementation, customer expectations, or commercial obligations.
3. The AE is asked to provide clarification or supporting documentation.
4. The CS Manager is notified if the missing information creates material implementation or customer risk.
5. The issue is tracked until resolved.

A missing field should not automatically delay every customer transition. However, unresolved contractual, technical, security, or scope issues must be escalated before they create customer-facing commitments.

## 7. Customer Kickoff & Initial Success Alignment

The kickoff should build on information already gathered during the sales process rather than repeat the original discovery conversation.

### Recommended Kickoff Agenda

| Agenda Item | Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introductions & Roles | Confirm stakeholders, responsibilities, and communication preferences. |
| Business Objectives | Reconfirm the problems the customer wants to solve. |
| Success Criteria | Establish measurable outcomes and initial indicators of progress. |
| Implementation Scope | Review purchased products, configuration requirements, and dependencies. |
| Timeline & Milestones | Agree on target dates, customer responsibilities, and internal deliverables. |
| Training & Enablement | Identify user groups, training requirements, and adoption needs. |
| Communication Cadence | Establish check-ins, escalation contacts, and reporting expectations. |
| Next Steps | Assign action items, owners, and due dates. |

### Initial Mutual Success Plan

The CSM should establish a baseline success plan covering:

- Customer business objectives.
- Target outcomes and success measurements.
- Implementation milestones.
- Customer and vendor responsibilities.
- Key stakeholders and decision-makers.
- Training and adoption activities.
- Outstanding dependencies.
- Risks and mitigation actions.
- Target dates and review cadence.

The plan should be shared with the customer and maintained throughout onboarding.

## 8. Post-Handoff CRM Documentation

Following kickoff, the CSM should update the account record to reflect the transition from Sales into active Customer Success management.

### Required Updates

- Confirm assigned CSM and implementation owner, where applicable.
- Record confirmed business objectives and success criteria.
- Update stakeholder and contact records.
- Document kickoff date and implementation milestones.
- Attach or link the Mutual Success Plan.
- Record outstanding technical dependencies.
- Document risks and mitigation plans.
- Create follow-up tasks with owners and due dates.
- Establish the next customer engagement date.
- Confirm the renewal date and appropriate renewal planning milestones.

### Account Ownership

| Function | Primary Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Account Executive | Commercial context, deal history, contract accuracy, and transition support. |
| Customer Success Manager | Relationship ownership, adoption, customer outcomes, health, and retention. |
| Implementation / Professional Services | Configuration, technical delivery, and implementation milestones, where applicable. |
| Support | Product issues, troubleshooting, and support ticket resolution. |
| CS Leadership | Assignment oversight, escalation support, and handoff process governance. |

Sales should remain available during the transition for clarification and relationship continuity. The CSM becomes the primary post-sale relationship owner according to the organization's account ownership model.

## 9. Handoff Exceptions & Escalation Triggers

Not every customer can follow the standard transition timeline. Complex enterprise accounts, urgent implementations, and incomplete sales cycles may require additional coordination.

| Scenario | Risk | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Incomplete CRM intake | Context loss and repeated discovery | Return missing information to AE and track outstanding fields. |
| Unvalidated product commitment | Scope conflict or customer dissatisfaction | Escalate to Product / Solutions Engineering for validation before confirming delivery. |
| Aggressive go-live deadline | Implementation failure | Review feasibility with Implementation and agree on a realistic timeline. |
| Missing Executive Sponsor | Weak strategic alignment | Identify an alternate sponsor and establish an executive engagement plan. |
| Unresolved technical dependency | Delayed implementation | Assign a technical owner and document required customer inputs. |
| Customer expectation mismatch | Early trust erosion | Align internally before communicating scope or timeline changes. |
| High-value or strategically sensitive account | Elevated business exposure | Involve CS leadership and establish an appropriate transition plan. |

### Escalation Guidelines

- **Level 1 — Operational:** Missing documentation, incomplete contact information, or routine scheduling gaps. Resolve between AE and CSM.
- **Level 2 — Implementation:** Technical dependencies, resource constraints, or scope questions affecting delivery. Involve Implementation, Solutions Engineering, or the appropriate functional lead.
- **Level 3 — Strategic / Commercial:** Contractual discrepancies, material customer expectation gaps, executive concerns, or significant revenue exposure. Involve Sales and CS leadership.

Escalations should have a documented owner, next action, and communication plan.

## 10. Handoff Performance Metrics

The handoff process should be measured to identify recurring breakdowns, improve cross-functional execution, and reduce delays between Closed-Won and customer value realization.

| KPI | Definition | Recommended Measurement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Handoff Completion Rate | Percentage of Closed-Won accounts meeting required handoff criteria. | Complete handoffs / Total Closed-Won accounts × 100 |
| Time to CSM Assignment | Time between Closed-Won and confirmed CSM assignment. | Average business hours or days. |
| Time to Internal Handoff | Time between Closed-Won and completed AE-to-CS alignment. | Average business hours or days. |
| Time to Customer Introduction | Time between Closed-Won and customer introduction. | Average business hours or days. |
| Kickoff Scheduling Time | Time between Closed-Won and confirmed kickoff date. | Average calendar or business days. |
| Handoff Data Completeness | Percentage of required CRM intake fields completed before handoff. | Completed required fields / Total required fields × 100 |
| Handoff-Related Rework | Number of onboarding delays or customer issues attributable to missing or inaccurate sales context. | Count and categorize recurring issues. |
| Early Implementation Risk | Percentage of new accounts entering implementation with unresolved critical dependencies. | Accounts with critical open dependencies / New accounts × 100 |

### Recommended Operating Targets

These are starting benchmarks to calibrate against actual team capacity and account complexity, not universal industry standards.

- 95%+ completion of required handoff fields.
- 90%+ of accounts assigned a CSM within one business day.
- 90%+ of internal handoffs completed within 48 business hours.
- 95%+ of customer introductions sent within two business days.
- 100% of identified critical risks assigned an owner and next action.

### Review Cadence

- **Weekly:** Review incomplete handoffs, unassigned accounts, and outstanding transition risks.
- **Monthly:** Analyze handoff completion, data quality, and recurring delays.
- **Quarterly:** Review trends with Sales and CS leadership and identify process improvements.

## 11. Continuous Improvement & Governance

The handoff process should evolve as the organization, product, customer base, and implementation model change.

### Ownership

- **Sales Leadership:** Ensures Account Executives complete the required commercial and customer context.
- **CS Leadership:** Owns the post-sale transition standard, CSM assignment process, and operational governance.
- **Implementation Leadership:** Validates technical readiness and implementation requirements.
- **Revenue Operations:** Supports CRM field structure, reporting, automation, and data quality where applicable.

### Quarterly Process Review

Review the following questions:

1. Are customers repeating information already shared during Sales?
2. Which CRM fields are most frequently incomplete?
3. Are Sales commitments consistently aligned with implementation capabilities?
4. Are CSMs receiving enough context to lead effective kickoffs?
5. Which handoff issues are causing onboarding delays?
6. Are account ownership and escalation responsibilities clear?
7. What changes would reduce friction between Sales and Customer Success?

Document agreed improvements, assign owners, and track progress through the next review cycle.

---

## 12. Operating Philosophy

**The customer should experience one continuous relationship, not two disconnected departments.**

Sales establishes the initial relationship and commercial expectations. Customer Success carries that relationship forward, translating the customer's original business objectives into measurable outcomes.

A strong handoff ensures that:

- The customer doesn't have to start over.
- The CSM understands why the customer bought.
- Commercial and technical commitments are visible.
- Stakeholders know who owns the next step.
- Implementation begins with clear expectations.
- Success is defined before adoption and renewal are evaluated.

**The sale is not the finish line. It is the beginning of the customer's journey toward measurable value.**
