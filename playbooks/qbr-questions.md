# Playbook: Enterprise QBR & Strategic Account Growth

**Owner:** Senior Customer Success Manager / Strategic Account Manager  
**Portfolio Scope:** Mid-Market & Enterprise B2B SaaS  
**Focus Areas:** Executive Alignment, Product Adoption, Business Reviews, Retention, Account Growth & Renewal Strategy

## Purpose

Establish a structured approach to Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs) that moves customer conversations beyond performance reporting and toward strategic alignment, measurable business outcomes, and long-term account growth.

QBRs should create opportunities to understand changing customer priorities, uncover adoption barriers, reinforce product value, and identify opportunities to strengthen the partnership.

My approach centers on five objectives:

1. Understand the customer's evolving business priorities.
2. Evaluate progress against previously established success metrics.
3. Identify adoption gaps and opportunities for improvement.
4. Reinforce measurable business value.
5. Align on future priorities, retention, and potential expansion.

**Operating Principle:** Data tells me where to look. Customer conversations tell me why.

---

## 1. QBR Preparation & Account Intelligence

A successful QBR begins before the meeting. Preparation ensures that conversations are relevant, data-informed, and aligned with the customer's business objectives.

### Pre-QBR Preparation Checklist

**Account Performance**
- Review product usage and adoption trends.
- Evaluate progress against established success metrics.
- Identify changes in engagement and utilization.
- Review customer health indicators.

**Customer Relationship**
- Confirm executive sponsor and key stakeholder participation.
- Review previous meeting notes and outstanding commitments.
- Identify changes in customer priorities or organizational structure.
- Evaluate stakeholder engagement and relationship health.

**Support & Operations**
- Review open support tickets and escalations.
- Identify recurring product or operational challenges.
- Evaluate resolution times and outstanding customer concerns.
- Identify opportunities for additional training or enablement.

**Growth & Renewal**
- Review contract terms and renewal timelines.
- Identify underutilized features and additional use cases.
- Evaluate seat utilization and potential expansion opportunities.
- Document potential risks to renewal.

**Deliverable:** A concise account brief highlighting performance, customer health, risks, opportunities, and recommended discussion points.

## 2. QBR Meeting Structure

A QBR should balance historical performance with forward-looking strategy.

| Section | Discussion Focus | Desired Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Business Alignment** | Customer priorities and organizational changes | Confirm strategic direction |
| **2. Performance Review** | Adoption, usage, and success metrics | Evaluate progress |
| **3. Challenges & Opportunities** | Adoption barriers, support issues, and workflow gaps | Identify improvement areas |
| **4. Value Realization** | Business impact and measurable outcomes | Reinforce product value |
| **5. Growth Strategy** | Additional use cases and expansion opportunities | Identify future potential |
| **6. Action Planning** | Priorities, owners, and timelines | Establish mutual accountability |

The objective is to ensure that every QBR produces actionable next steps rather than simply reviewing historical metrics.

## 3. QBR Questions That Drive Adoption

Quarterly Business Reviews aren't just about reporting numbers. They're about creating alignment, uncovering opportunities, and ensuring customer goals remain connected to product value.

These are the core questions I use to guide conversations and drive meaningful outcomes.

### Business Priorities

**1. What outcome matters most this quarter?**

Usually framed as a single KPI the customer would proudly show their leadership team.

**2. Have your business priorities or internal goals changed since our last review?**

Helps identify organizational changes that may affect product usage, implementation priorities, or success criteria.

### Adoption & Engagement

**3. What's blocking broader adoption today?**

Digging into people, process, or technical barriers.

**4. Which features did you try but pause? What would bring you back?**

Reveals friction points, unmet expectations, or gaps in enablement.

**5. Are there teams or departments that could benefit from broader platform adoption?**

Identifies opportunities to increase utilization and extend product value.

### Operational Efficiency & Growth

**6. Which workflows are still manual and should be automated?**

Helps surface opportunities for process improvement and potential expansion.

**7. Are there additional use cases where our platform could deliver value?**

Encourages a forward-looking conversation around customer needs and growth opportunities.

### Retention & Relationship Health

**8. What concerns could prevent us from achieving our goals together?**

Creates space for honest feedback before issues become renewal risks.

**9. What's the one thing we could do to earn a glowing renewal?**

Puts the conversation squarely on value and retention.

**10. What would make this partnership significantly more valuable over the next 90 days?**

Establishes a shared vision for the next phase of the relationship.

## 4. Connecting Product Adoption to Business Value

Product usage alone does not demonstrate customer success. The objective is to understand how adoption translates into meaningful operational or financial outcomes.

### Example Performance Review

| Metric | Previous Quarter | Current Quarter | Strategic Discussion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Active Users | 65% | 78% | What drove increased engagement? |
| Feature Adoption | 45% | 62% | Which capabilities are delivering the most value? |
| Workflow Completion | 70% | 85% | What operational improvements contributed to the increase? |
| Support Tickets | 24 | 15 | Have recurring issues been resolved? |
| Seat Utilization | 72% | 88% | Are additional seats or departments needed? |

*Illustrative data for demonstrating how to structure a QBR discussion.*

### Turning Metrics Into Conversations

Rather than simply presenting a percentage, connect the result to the customer's experience.

For example:

- Adoption increased: What changed within the organization?
- Support tickets decreased: Which improvements had the greatest impact?
- Seat utilization increased: Is the customer approaching capacity?
- Feature adoption remains low: Is there a training or workflow barrier?

The goal is to move from reporting what happened to understanding why it happened and deciding what comes next.

## 5. Identifying Expansion Opportunities

QBRs provide a natural opportunity to uncover additional customer needs, but expansion should follow demonstrated value.

### Expansion Signals

| Signal | Potential Opportunity | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| High Seat Utilization | Additional licenses | Explore team growth and capacity requirements |
| Strong Feature Adoption | Additional product capabilities | Identify complementary use cases |
| Manual Workflows | Automation opportunities | Conduct workflow discovery |
| Department-Level Success | Broader organizational rollout | Engage additional stakeholders |
| New Business Priorities | Additional product applications | Revisit success objectives |

### Expansion Conversation Framework

1. Understand the customer's current operational challenges.
2. Identify gaps in existing workflows.
3. Determine whether additional product capabilities could address those gaps.
4. Quantify potential business value where possible.
5. Align with the appropriate stakeholders before introducing a commercial opportunity.

Expansion should feel like a natural extension of customer success, not an unrelated sales pitch.

## 6. Executive Stakeholder Alignment

Enterprise relationships require engagement beyond the primary day-to-day contact.

A strong QBR should ensure that the executive sponsor, internal champion, and operational stakeholders share an understanding of the partnership's value.

| Stakeholder | Primary Interest | QBR Discussion Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Executive Sponsor** | Business outcomes and ROI | Strategic objectives and measurable impact |
| **Primary Champion** | Adoption and internal success | Engagement, enablement, and workflow improvements |
| **Technical Stakeholders** | Reliability and integration | Technical performance and outstanding dependencies |
| **Department Leaders** | Team efficiency and productivity | Adoption across business units |
| **Procurement / Finance** | Contract value and utilization | Renewal readiness and commercial alignment |

My approach is to build relationships across the account rather than relying exclusively on one contact.

**Operating Principle:** Don't chase a contact; build the relationship with the account.

## 7. Post-QBR Follow-Through & Accountability

The value of a QBR is determined by what happens after the meeting.

Every review should conclude with documented decisions, clear ownership, and agreed-upon next steps.

### Post-QBR Action Plan

| Action Item | Owner | Timeline | Success Measure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Deliver additional user training | CSM / Customer Champion | 14 Days | Improved feature adoption |
| Resolve outstanding support issue | Support / Operations | 7 Days | Issue resolution |
| Evaluate automation opportunity | CSM / Customer Team | 30 Days | Documented workflow assessment |
| Review expansion requirements | Account Manager / CSM | 30 Days | Qualified opportunity |
| Reassess adoption performance | CSM | Next Monthly Review | Improved usage trends |

### Documentation Standards

Following every QBR:

- Update CRM account notes.
- Record stakeholder feedback and sentiment.
- Document business priorities and success metrics.
- Assign owners to outstanding commitments.
- Update customer health and risk indicators where appropriate.
- Record qualified expansion opportunities.
- Schedule follow-up meetings and future reviews.

## 8. Measuring QBR Effectiveness

QBR effectiveness should be measured by its influence on customer engagement, adoption, retention, and account growth.

| KPI | What It Measures |
| :--- | :--- |
| **QBR Completion Rate** | Percentage of targeted accounts completing scheduled reviews |
| **Action Item Completion** | Percentage of QBR commitments completed within agreed timelines |
| **Product Adoption** | Changes in feature utilization and active engagement |
| **Stakeholder Engagement** | Participation and alignment across customer decision-makers |
| **Customer Health** | Changes in overall account health and relationship sentiment |
| **Renewal Performance** | Retention outcomes across reviewed accounts |
| **Expansion Pipeline** | Qualified growth opportunities identified through strategic reviews |

QBRs should not be judged solely by attendance or presentation quality. Their effectiveness comes from the decisions they generate and the customer outcomes that follow.

---

## 9. Strategic Operating Philosophy

My approach to QBRs is built around six principles:

1. **Prepare:** Understand the account before entering the conversation.
2. **Align:** Connect customer priorities to measurable success outcomes.
3. **Diagnose:** Use data and conversations to identify adoption barriers.
4. **Demonstrate:** Reinforce business value through meaningful performance insights.
5. **Expand:** Identify opportunities that align with evolving customer needs.
6. **Execute:** Convert discussions into documented commitments and measurable actions.

**Bottom Line:** The best QBRs aren't presentations about what happened last quarter. They're strategic conversations about what the customer wants to accomplish next, how we can help them get there, and why the partnership continues to matter.
