# Playbook: At-Risk Account Recovery & Customer Retention

**Owner:** Senior Customer Success Manager / Strategic Account Manager  
**Portfolio Scope:** Mid-Market & Enterprise B2B SaaS  
**Focus Areas:** Churn Prevention, Customer Escalation, Relationship Recovery, Retention & Account Growth

## Purpose

Establish a structured approach to identifying, diagnosing, and recovering at-risk customer relationships before service issues, declining adoption, or unresolved business challenges develop into customer churn.

The objective is to stabilize customer relationships, restore confidence, reinforce measurable business value, and protect long-term retention while identifying opportunities for continued account growth.

My approach follows a simple principle:

**Stabilize the relationship before trying to expand it.**

---

## 1. Identifying At-Risk Customers

Customer churn rarely happens overnight. In many cases, there are early indicators that customer expectations are no longer being met.

I focus on identifying changes in customer behavior, product engagement, operational performance, and stakeholder relationships.

| Risk Indicator | Potential Warning Sign | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Declining Adoption** | Reduced product usage or engagement with key features | Review adoption trends and identify barriers |
| **Service Issues** | Repeated support escalations or unresolved operational problems | Identify root causes and coordinate internal resolution |
| **Stakeholder Disengagement** | Primary champion becomes unresponsive or stops attending meetings | Reestablish communication and identify additional stakeholders |
| **Negative Sentiment** | Customer expresses frustration or declining confidence | Schedule a direct conversation to understand concerns |
| **Renewal Risk** | Customer questions product value or contract continuation | Revisit business objectives and demonstrate measurable outcomes |
| **Reduced Account Activity** | Declining transaction volume, utilization, or engagement | Investigate changes in business needs and usage patterns |

Risk indicators should be evaluated in context. A decline in usage does not automatically indicate churn, just as a healthy usage dashboard does not guarantee a healthy customer relationship.

---

## 2. Diagnosing the Root Cause

Before attempting to solve a problem, I focus on understanding what is actually driving customer dissatisfaction.

The objective is to distinguish the visible symptom from the underlying business problem.

### Diagnostic Framework

**1. Understand the Customer's Perspective**

- What specifically is frustrating the customer?
- How has the issue affected their business?
- What expectations are currently not being met?
- Has the customer experienced similar problems previously?

**2. Review Account History**

- Previous support escalations.
- Product adoption and engagement trends.
- Open issues and unresolved commitments.
- Historical customer feedback.
- Renewal timelines and stakeholder changes.

**3. Identify the Root Cause**

Determine whether the primary concern is related to product functionality, service delivery, operational execution, communication, adoption, or misaligned expectations.

**4. Establish Priorities**

Separate immediate issues requiring urgent intervention from longer-term improvements that can be addressed through a structured success plan.

**Operating Principle:** Data tells me where to look. Customer conversations tell me why.

---

## 3. Escalation & Recovery Strategy

Once the underlying issue is understood, I establish a coordinated recovery plan with clear ownership, communication expectations, and measurable milestones.

### Recovery Framework

| Phase | Action | Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Acknowledge** | Listen, validate the customer's concerns, and recognize the business impact | Rebuild trust and demonstrate accountability |
| **2. Investigate** | Review account history and identify the root cause | Establish a clear understanding of the problem |
| **3. Escalate** | Engage the appropriate internal teams, including Support, Operations, Product, or Engineering | Get the right resources involved |
| **4. Stabilize** | Establish immediate corrective actions and contingency plans | Minimize ongoing customer disruption |
| **5. Communicate** | Provide proactive updates and maintain clear expectations | Restore customer confidence |
| **6. Validate** | Confirm that the issue has been resolved and the customer recognizes the improvement | Ensure recovery is meaningful |
| **7. Sustain** | Monitor performance and maintain ongoing engagement | Prevent recurring issues and protect retention |

### Internal Escalation Management

Every escalation should have:

- A clearly identified issue owner.
- Defined responsibilities across internal teams.
- An agreed-upon resolution timeline.
- Documented customer commitments.
- A communication cadence.
- A process for confirming resolution.

I use the CRM and support platforms to maintain visibility into open issues, track follow-up activities, and ensure important customer commitments do not get lost between departments.

---

## 4. Customer Communication During Recovery

Communication is often just as important as the resolution itself.

Customers should never have to repeatedly chase their CSM for updates on an issue affecting their business.

### Communication Standards

**Acknowledge Quickly**

Recognize the issue and its business impact. Avoid becoming defensive or making promises before understanding the situation.

**Set Clear Expectations**

Explain what is being investigated, which teams are involved, and when the customer can expect another update.

**Communicate Proactively**

Provide updates even when there is no major development. Silence creates uncertainty and can further damage customer confidence.

**Close the Loop**

Confirm when the issue has been resolved, explain what changed, and verify that the customer is comfortable moving forward.

**Maintain the Relationship**

Continue regular engagement after the immediate issue is resolved. A successful recovery should lead to stronger customer alignment, not simply the closure of a support ticket.

## 5. The 30-Day Customer Recovery Plan

For accounts experiencing significant customer dissatisfaction, I use a structured recovery timeline to establish accountability and measure progress.

| Timeline | Priority | Key Activities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Days 1–5** | Understand & Stabilize | Engage customer, acknowledge concerns, investigate root cause, and establish escalation ownership |
| **Days 6–10** | Execute | Coordinate internal teams, implement corrective actions, and provide proactive customer updates |
| **Days 11–20** | Validate | Review performance improvements, confirm issue resolution, and gather customer feedback |
| **Days 21–30** | Sustain | Reestablish success milestones, monitor account health, and reinforce ongoing value |

### Recovery Success Indicators

- Resolution of critical customer issues.
- Improved customer sentiment and stakeholder engagement.
- Restoration of product adoption or operational performance.
- Completion of outstanding commitments.
- Increased confidence in the ongoing partnership.
- Improved renewal outlook.

The timeline should be adjusted based on the severity of the issue, customer expectations, and operational complexity.

---

## 6. Retention, Renewal & Expansion

Once the customer relationship has stabilized, the focus shifts toward reinforcing value and strengthening the long-term partnership.

I approach this in three stages.

**1. Restore Confidence**

Demonstrate that the organization understands the customer's challenges and has taken meaningful action to address them.

**2. Reinforce Business Value**

Revisit the customer's original objectives, review performance improvements, and identify areas where the product or service is delivering value.

**3. Identify Growth Opportunities**

Once trust and operational stability have been reestablished, explore additional use cases, increased utilization, expanded service requirements, or opportunities to support other business units.

Expansion should be driven by demonstrated customer value rather than simply attempting to increase contract size.

---

## 7. Measuring Recovery Performance

A successful recovery should be measured by more than whether the customer renews.

I evaluate both operational improvements and the strength of the customer relationship.

| KPI | What It Measures |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Issue Resolution Time** | Time required to resolve critical customer issues |
| **Customer Sentiment** | Changes in customer feedback and relationship confidence |
| **Product Adoption** | Recovery in usage, engagement, and feature utilization |
| **Stakeholder Engagement** | Reestablishment of communication with key decision-makers and champions |
| **Renewal Rate** | Percentage of at-risk accounts successfully retained |
| **Revenue Retention** | Revenue preserved through successful customer recovery |
| **Expansion Revenue** | Additional business generated after customer confidence is restored |

The goal is to identify whether the recovery process is producing sustainable improvements rather than temporary customer satisfaction.

## 8. Real-World Case Study: Uber Freight Customer Turnaround

**Context:** Enterprise Shipper Account | Uber Freight

A shipper was experiencing repeated carrier delays that negatively affected their business operations. The recurring service disruptions created frustration and placed the customer relationship at risk.

### The Challenge

The customer needed more consistent shipment execution and greater confidence that operational issues would be addressed before they continued affecting their business.

### Actions Taken

**1. Listened & Acknowledged**

Actively listened to the customer's concerns and acknowledged the operational impact of the repeated delays in their own words.

**2. Documented & Escalated**

Logged the issue in Zendesk to establish visibility and coordinate communication across the appropriate internal teams, including Operations and Engineering.

**3. Coordinated Operational Recovery**

Partnered with Operations to secure backup carriers and stabilize shipment execution.

**4. Established Proactive Communication**

Provided daily customer updates throughout the recovery process to maintain transparency, communicate progress, and reinforce accountability.

### Outcome

- Stabilized shipment execution through backup carrier coordination.
- Rebuilt customer confidence through consistent communication and follow-through.
- Successfully retained the customer through contract renewal.
- Expanded customer volume by **15%**.

### Key Takeaway

The experience reinforced an important principle: customer retention is not just about resolving an individual issue. It is about restoring confidence in the partnership.

By combining active listening, cross-functional coordination, operational problem-solving, and proactive communication, I was able to help turn a frustrated customer relationship into a stronger long-term business opportunity.

## 9. Strategic Operating Philosophy

My approach to customer recovery is built around six principles:

1. **Understand:** Listen to the customer and understand the actual business impact.
2. **Diagnose:** Identify the underlying issue rather than treating symptoms.
3. **Prioritize:** Determine what needs immediate attention and establish clear ownership.
4. **Engage:** Maintain proactive communication and rebuild stakeholder confidence.
5. **Act:** Coordinate internal resources and execute a measurable recovery plan.
6. **Sustain:** Validate improvements, reinforce customer value, and protect the long-term relationship.

**Bottom Line:** Retention starts before renewal. The strongest Customer Success professionals recognize when a relationship is at risk, take ownership of the recovery process, and turn difficult customer experiences into opportunities to build stronger partnerships.
